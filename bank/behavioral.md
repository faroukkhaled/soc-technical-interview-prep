# Behavioural and project questions

### Q: Tell me about a project you did.
- domain: behavioral
- level: 1
- keywords: goal, built, detection, learned
A: Use the STAR shape: goal, what you built, one detection or decision in detail, what you learned. For example, the AD and Sysmon lab: you built a Sigma-style engine that runs 19 rules, scored them against a labelled simulation for missed detections and false positives, and wrote tests. Be honest that the data is synthetic and be ready to explain the code.

### Q: How do you stay current with threats?
- domain: behavioral
- level: 1
- keywords: advisories, blogs, CVE, labs
A: Name real sources you use: vendor advisories and CISA, DFIR and threat intel blogs, CVE feeds, practising in labs, and communities. Give a recent example and what you changed because of it.

### Q: Describe a time you made a mistake and how you handled it.
- domain: behavioral
- level: 1
- keywords: process, mistake, fix, blame
A: Pick a real, small-to-moderate mistake. Own it, explain the immediate fix, and describe the process change that prevents it. Do not blame others.

### Q: How do you prioritise when five alerts arrive at once?
- domain: behavioral
- level: 2
- keywords: severity, asset criticality, user, SLA
A: By severity and confidence, asset criticality and user role, whether money or data is moving, and the SLA. Handle active compromise and critical assets first, ask for help when the queue exceeds capacity, and document what was deferred and why.

### Q: How do you explain a security incident to a non-technical manager?
- domain: behavioral
- level: 2
- keywords: impact, timeline, status, jargon
A: Start with impact and current status, then what you did, what you need decided, and the next update time. Avoid jargon, separate confirmed facts from assumptions, and give a short timeline.

### Q: Why do you want to work in a SOC?
- domain: behavioral
- level: 1
- keywords: detection, investigation, labs, bank
A: Give a specific reason: you enjoy investigation and detection work, and you have already built labs and written detections. Link it to the employer's environment (for example protecting customers and money in a bank).

### Q: What is your experience with offensive security and how does it help a defender?
- domain: behavioral
- level: 2
- keywords: attacker, detection, purple, certifications
A: Offensive experience helps you think like the attacker: you know what artefacts techniques leave and where defences are weak. Describe how you would turn an attack technique into a detection and test it with a purple-team exercise. Mention only certifications you actually hold.
